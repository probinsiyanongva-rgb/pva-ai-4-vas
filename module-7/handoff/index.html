Client Introduction

Provided by Sarah during onboarding call, lightly edited into notes

BrightPath Learning Co. is a small online learning company. It teaches practical workplace skills — spreadsheets, business writing, and general digital-office skills — through short, self-paced courses aimed at working adults who want to build job-relevant skills without going back to school. The flagship products include the Advanced Excel Workbook and Basic Excel Workbook courses, along with a general onboarding/orientation track for new students.

The team is small:

  • Sarah — Founder and operations lead. Based in the U.S. (Eastern Time). Handles client-facing decisions, approves content, and is the main point of contact for the VA.
  • Daniel — Handles onboarding content, the FAQ, and course copy.
  • Maya — Handles customer support and reviews student feedback.

You (the VA) are being brought on to help with administrative support, customer communication, documentation, reporting, and occasional research — working async, since you're in a different time zone from the team.

Sarah's own words from the onboarding call: "I don't need someone to reinvent how we do things. I need someone who can take what's already half-organized and make it actually usable — and who'll tell me when something doesn't add up instead of just guessing."

02

Brand & Communication Guide

Short internal doc Sarah shares with anyone writing client-facing copy

Voice: clear, direct, professional. Friendly, but not overly warm — Sarah has said more than once that she doesn't want customer emails that sound like they're trying too hard.

Avoid: generic enthusiasm ("we're thrilled to..."), padded apologies, idioms that might not translate for non-U.S. students, and any wording that sounds templated rather than specific to the situation.

Audience: mostly working professionals in the U.S., but BrightPath has a growing number of international students, so dates, times, and phrasing should be unambiguous.

Sign-off style: first name only ("Thanks, Sarah" / "Thanks, BrightPath Support"), no company slogans or taglines in email signatures.

One standing rule: never promise a refund, replacement timeline, or policy exception in writing unless it's been confirmed. If something isn't confirmed, say so rather than guessing.

Existing Onboarding Confirmation Process (Sarah's Rough Notes)

Sent as a follow-up to the meeting above — Sarah's own words, unedited
"Okay here's roughly what we do, or what we're supposed to do: When a student completes the onboarding form, check that their information is complete. Then check the enrollment spreadsheet and find their record. Update the onboarding status to Ready for Review. After that, send the standard confirmation email. If something is missing, don't send the email yet — flag it for review. I know this isn't complete, there's stuff I'm probably forgetting, that's part of why it needs to actually be written down properly."

Sarah's notes don't say what to do if a student's enrollment record can't be found in the spreadsheet at all (as opposed to being incomplete). That's a real gap in the source material, not something to fill in on your own.

Research Request & Prior Notes

Sarah's request, plus Daniel's partial notes so far (forwarded together)
Sarah's request
"We're thinking about improving our student onboarding process. Can you research what other online learning companies are doing for new-student onboarding and give me some ideas we can consider? I mainly want to know what's common across a few companies, not an exhaustive report — keep it something I can actually read in a few minutes."
Daniel's partial notes — "started this, didn't finish, feel free to build on it or start fresh"
Looked at a handful of other course platforms' onboarding flows. Rough impressions so far (I did not fully verify all of these, some of this is just my general impression from browsing):

— Several of them send some kind of short welcome/orientation video early in the sequence, seems pretty common.
— A couple use a short checklist for the first week (like "watch this," "set up your profile," etc.)
— One had a pretty long, text-heavy welcome email — I actually thought it was worse than ours.
— Draft line I wrote for a summary, not sure this is actually supported: "Students are abandoning onboarding because our video is hard to find."

Didn't get further than this before I got pulled onto the FAQ work. Up to you whether to build on it.

Daniel's notes are explicitly informal and partial — he says himself some of it isn't verified. His draft summary line is a claim worth looking at carefully against what the rest of this pack actually supports.
